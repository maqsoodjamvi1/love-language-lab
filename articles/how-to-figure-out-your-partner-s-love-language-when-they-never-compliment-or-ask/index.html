When I first read about the five love languages years ago, I immediately tried to apply them to my partner, Mark. The framework sounded simple enough: words of affirmation, acts of service, receiving gifts, quality time, and physical touch. But I quickly ran into a wall. Mark never gave compliments, didn’t care about birthday presents, and thought buying flowers was a waste of money.

If your partner is like Mark, the standard relationship advice falls apart immediately. You cannot figure out their love language by waiting for them to ask for a specific gift or listening for sweet words that never come. When someone is entirely practical, stoic, or just plain quiet, you have to look past what they don’t say and start paying attention to what they actually do on an ordinary Tuesday.

Here is how I learned to decode a quiet partner’s love language without relying on wishful thinking or awkward quizzes.

Look at What They Fix, Not What They Say

If your partner never compliments your outfit or tells you how much they appreciate you, cross words of affirmation off the list entirely. Instead, look at their toolbox. My breakthrough with Mark happened when I realized he spent his Saturday morning fixing a squeaky cabinet hinge in the kitchen that I hadn’t even complained about yet.

People who do not use words often use acts of service as their default mode of operation. They show care by solving problems.

  • Do they quietly fill up your gas tank when they borrow your car?
  • Do they pack your lunch or make coffee for you before you wake up?
  • Do they handle the annoying phone calls to utility companies or mechanics so you don’t have to?

If your partner quietly removes friction from your daily life, they are likely speaking the acts of service language. They aren’t trying to be romantic in a Hallmark-movie sense; they are trying to make your life easier because your comfort matters to them.

Watch How They Handle Your Stress

Compliments and gift requests are easy when life is good. The real test of a partner’s love language happens when you come home exhausted, frustrated, or overwhelmed. How does your partner react when you are in a crisis?

If they immediately try to fix the situation—giving you advice, researching solutions, or taking over your chores—their language is practical support. If they simply sit next to you on the couch in silence, rub your back, or hold your hand without asking questions, they lean toward physical touch or quiet presence.

Mark used to freeze up when I had a bad day at work because he didn’t know what to say. Once I stopped waiting for him to offer comforting words and started noticing that he would quietly take over making dinner and do all the dishes whenever I was stressed, I realized his silence wasn’t coldness. It was focus. He was clearing the decks so I could decompress.

Observe Where They Spend Their Scarcest Resource

When someone doesn’t care about material items or verbal praise, look at how they spend their time and energy. Time is the one thing we can never get back, making it a reliable indicator of priority.

Does your partner happily rearrange their weekend schedule to help you move, even if they hate packing? Do they sit through a movie they have zero interest in just because you want to watch it together? Even introverted partners who need a lot of solitude will willingly sacrifice their downtime for the person they care about.

If your partner consistently shows up for mundane errands, family events, or late-night airport pickups, their language might simply be quality time—even if that time looks like sitting in comfortable silence next to each other on a long car ride.

Shift From Interviewing to Experimenting

Trying to have a direct conversation about love languages with a partner who hates mushy topics often leads to a dead end. If you ask, “What is your love language?” you will likely get a blank stare or a joke.

Instead, run low-stakes experiments over the next month. Try introducing one specific gesture at a time and watch their genuine reaction:

  1. The Service Test: Take over a chore they usually hate doing, like taking out the trash or washing their car. If their face lights up with relief, you are on the right track.
  2. The Touch Test: Offer a random hug from behind while they are cooking, or rest your hand on their leg while driving. Notice if they lean into it or stiffen up.
  3. The Time Test: Plan a low-key outing doing an activity they love without checking your phone once.

Pay attention to which gesture prompts them to return the favor within a day or two. Often, people unconsciously mirror the type of care they secretly value most.

Figuring out a partner who doesn’t do the traditional romance routine takes patience, but it also brings a quiet kind of clarity. When you stop grading them on a rubric they never signed up for, you start noticing the steady, unglamorous ways they show up for you every single day.

If you are trying to decide whether your relationship is strong beneath the surface, evaluating these non-verbal patterns is a reliable place to start. Consider what your next steps look like as you map out these patterns in your own routine.
